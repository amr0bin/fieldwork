# Fieldwork — The Discovery Pass
### The move that comes before the template
*A working instrument · pairs with the Article Template v2 (structure), the Writing Quality & Reader Experience guide (craft), and the Qualities We're Writing Toward digest (principles). This is the layer above all three: it decides what a week is *about* before any of them are applied.*

---

## Why this exists

The weeks that felt alive and the weeks that felt assembled did not differ in effort. They differed in whether the week was built around one live idea or filled into a template. *The Generous Blur* worked because a turn was found first — "not your care, your blur" — and the image, the case, the subheads, even the photograph were all recruited to serve it. The flatter weeks are correct: the pillars are present, the case is cited, the carry-question lands. But they were produced by the template rather than discovered before it, and the reader feels the difference as the absence of a pulse.

The Discovery Pass makes that discovery a step instead of an accident. It runs first, every week, before the Article Template — roughly twenty to thirty minutes. It produces five things and, crucially, tells you **how hot to run each one for this particular week**. That calibration is the whole point: four techniques applied evenly would just be a new formula. Applied to what the week actually calls for, they stay alive.

---

## The five dials

### 1 · The turn — *always on full*

The one idea the reader does not have when they start and does have when they finish: the sentence that reorganizes something they thought they understood. *The Generous Blur*: the paralysis you feel is not too much care, it is too much blur, and you can give up one without giving up the other.

- **The rule.** If you cannot say the turn in a single sentence, you are not ready to draft. Freewrite until you can. This is non-negotiable, because it is the pulse; the other four dials only carry a turn that already exists.
- **The test.** Does it surprise *you* a little? If you already knew it flatly, it is a topic, not a turn. Keep digging until the week teaches you something you did not sit down knowing.
- **Where it fails quietly.** A week with no turn still *looks* finished — that is the trap. The symptom is that the piece could be summarized without loss. A real turn cannot be summarized away, because the reorganization *is* the content.

### 2 · The controlling image — *hot on dry weeks, light on vivid ones*

One image capable of carrying the turn structurally rather than decorating it. Blur and focus was not an illustration in *The Generous Blur*; it organized the argument and even chose the photograph. A controlling image runs into the case, the subheads, and the close.

- **Calibration.** Concept-dense weeks — legal frameworks, WCAG, methodology — need the image running *hot*, because the ideas are abstract and the image is what keeps them from going dry. Weeks that already carry a vivid human situation need only a *light* image, or one competes with the other and the reader feels handled.
- **The accretion system (phase governing images).** So images build across the year instead of resetting weekly, each of the four phases gets one governing image, and each week's image ladders up to it. A proposal, to finalize together:
  - **Phase 1 — Foundations & thesis discovery:** *from the vast to the holdable* — bringing boundless care into a shape a real person can carry through real weeks. (Week 3 the road that carries and drops; Week 4 blur into focus; Week 5 what you can carry the whole way — these already ladder.)
  - **Phase 2 — Literature & scaffolding:** *the floor, the grammar, the compass, the method* — the tools you are handed and what each one can and cannot do. (Already latent in the series map's week titles.)
  - **Phase 3 — Investigation:** *nearness* — research as proximity rather than distance, the clipboard set down. (From the anchor essay's "research as nearness.")
  - **Phase 4 — Analysis, design & synthesis:** *giving it back* — the return of what was gathered, in a usable form, to the community it came from.
  - These are a starting proposal, not a decision. The value is that once set, each week's image has somewhere to belong.

### 3 · The pillar braid — *calibrate which pillar leads*

All three pillars are present every week, but never evenly. One leads; the other two support. Naming the lead before drafting is what stops the three from sitting side by side like a checklist — the single most common way a week goes flat.

| The week is really about… | Lead pillar | How the others support |
|---|---|---|
| What the law/standard requires and misses | Accessibility | DJ asks who it still fails; EL asks how practice adapts past it |
| Who the inquiry names, who leads it, whose voice | Disability Justice | Accessibility as the floor DJ moves past; EL as how leadership becomes method |
| How learning adapts, emerges, stays relational | Emergent Learning | Accessibility as precondition; DJ as who the adaptation is for |
| Consent, ethics, the relationship with participants | Disability Justice | Accessibility as baseline dignity; EL as consent-that-changes-over-time |

### 4 · The spine — *heavy on hinge weeks, light otherwise*

A real inquiry the reader can follow across the whole arc — so the year has narrative continuity and you have something to develop rather than restart weekly. Some weeks advance it heavily (a question narrows, hits its ethics wall, is prototyped, fails, iterates); most weeks only touch it.

- **The values guardrail.** Any followed case must be real, named, publicly sourced, and credited — never extractive, never a person turned into an occasion. Where a running illustration is composite or hypothetical, it is labelled as such, exactly as the fact-check standard already requires. This is a real conversation to have before committing: which published inquiries can bear being followed for a year.
- **Calibration.** Hinge weeks (the research question, the ethics turn, the prototype, the public iteration, the return) carry the spine *heavily*. Framework weeks touch it *lightly* — one sentence connecting the week's tool to where the followed inquiry currently stands.

### 5 · You, visible — *mostly off; on only at true thresholds*

The project is *about* practitioner-research and emergent learning, so the writing occasionally modeling inquiry — a question genuinely unsettled, a place a week resisted being written — is not indulgence; it is the method enacted. It is also where *your* excitement lives, because it gives you room to be a mind in the work rather than its executor.

- **The dose.** Off by default: most weeks the method speaks and you stay invisible. On only where the work is honestly hard or uncertain — a handful of weeks a year. Overused, it becomes exactly the kind of tic the craft guide warns against; the restraint is what gives it weight when it appears.

## The worksheet — the twenty-minute pass

Answer these six before opening the template. If a prompt stalls, freewrite it; the stall is usually where the turn is hiding.

1. **Say the turn in one sentence.** The thing the reader doesn't have at the start and does by the end. If you can't yet, freewrite until the sentence surprises you.
2. **What single image carries it?** And does it ladder to the phase's governing image?
3. **Which pillar leads this week — and how do the other two support?** Name all three roles in one line each.
4. **Does the spine move this week? By how much?** One sentence on where the followed inquiry now stands.
5. **Is this a threshold where you should be visible?** If yes, write the one honest sentence of where you actually are with this. If no, stay out.
6. **Which door does the turn open?** (From the craft guide's door menu — the sincere object, the single moment of friction, the two rooms, the invisible default made visible.)

## Worked demonstration — Week 5, *Ethical constraints as design constraints*

Running the pass on a week that could easily have been a dry logistics post.

**1 · The turn.** Feasibility is not where ambition goes to die. It is the first ethical decision you make — because a study you cannot finish is a promise you break to the people who agreed to be carried by it.

**2 · The image.** *What you can carry the whole way.* A study is a thing you pick up and carry for twenty-four weeks; overload it and you set it down partway, and you set down the people inside it. Ladders directly to the Phase 1 governing image (*from the vast to the holdable*) and to the series' existing carriage motif.

**3 · The braid.** Leads with **Disability Justice** — consent as an ongoing relationship, not a one-time form; the ethics of not abandoning people mid-inquiry. **Accessibility** supports as the baseline of a scope that doesn't exhaust the researcher into dropping it. **Emergent Learning** supports as consent-that-changes: what was fine in week two may not be in week ten.

**4 · The spine.** Medium. The followed inquiry, having narrowed its question last week, now meets its first real constraint and has to scope down — the reader watches an honest researcher choose what to leave out.

**5 · You, visible.** Warm — a lived-interaction week. One honest line is available: scoping down is a small grief, and pretending it isn't would be its own dishonesty.

**6 · The door.** The sincere object — the well-meant, careful consent *form* that does exactly what it was designed to do and still fails the person.

**The opening this produces:**

> There is a version of this week that feels like a demotion. You have your question now — named, pointed at the person your system drops most reliably. And the next thing anyone says is: be realistic. Count your hours. Look at your budget, which is probably nothing. Be honest about the access you actually have. It sounds like the week ambition gets cut down to size.
>
> It isn't. Feasibility is the first ethical decision you make.
>
> A research question is a thing you pick up and carry — for twenty-four weeks, through your real job and your real life, alongside everyone who agreed to be carried with it. And the cruelest thing you can do to a participant is not to refuse them at the start. It is to begin, take their time and their trust, and then set the whole thing down in week fourteen because the hours were never there to finish. A study you cannot complete is a promise broken to the people most used to being dropped.
>
> Which is why consent, done honestly, was never a form. A form is a moment: a signature that transfers permission once and files it. But the people this inquiry is built for have most often met consent as exactly that — a document that asked once, took what it needed, and never came back. Consent as a relationship asks differently. It assumes the person may change their mind; that what was fine in week two may not be fine in week ten; that the right to withdraw has to stay warm and reachable the whole way through.

*Case to source before publishing:* Week 5's slot calls for a community-led feasibility or participatory-consent process — real, named, publicly accessible, with any figure traceable to a page. Draft the opening, the conceptual *Why*, and the *Close* freely (per the fact-check standard); leave the case as a marked placeholder until verified.

---

*Run this pass first, every week. The template still does its job — it just stops having to invent the pulse, because the pulse arrives before it.*
