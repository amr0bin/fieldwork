# Fieldwork — Participant Value & Artifact Map
### What each week gives the participant, what it enables them to do, and what they walk away holding
**From Accessible Learning Labs · Companion view to the 24-week syllabus and learning outcomes**

*The same visibility Groundwork gives at the module level, now mapped across all 24 weeks of Fieldwork.*

---

## How to read this

Each week names three things:

- **What the participant gains** — the concept or capacity they take on.
- **What it enables in practice** — the concrete move they can now make in their own work.
- **Portfolio artifact** — the real thing they produce that week.

**A note on the portfolio model.** Unlike Groundwork, where each module yields a standalone piece for an employer, Fieldwork's weekly artifacts are *components of one accumulating research dossier*. Week by week they build into a coherent body of work — a thesis plus a tested, born-accessible learning intervention — told end to end. That through-line is the point: the portfolio proves the participant can run a real inquiry from problem to public resource, accessibly, in a high-stakes context. The artifact column below is the trail; Section 6 shows how it rolls up.

## Phase 1 — Foundations & Thesis Discovery (Weeks 1–6)
*Goal: align personal interest with the thesis and establish the research problem.*

| Week | What the participant gains | What it enables in practice | Portfolio artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — The gap between policy and practice** | Learns to locate the precise place where official accessibility policy and lived learner experience diverge, and to treat it as researchable, not just frustrating | Can name a concrete, high-stakes problem in their own setting with a defensible point of view — the first move from practitioner to practitioner-researcher | **Statement of Intent** — names the phenomenon, their positionality, and the context that makes it urgent, plus a policy-vs-experience comparison locating the gap |
| **2 — Deconstructing the hypothesis** | Sees the three pillars (accessibility, disability justice, emergent learning) operating — or siloed — in their own environment | Can diagnose whether their setting integrates or isolates the pillars, and name whose exclusion is written into the "legal floor" | **Three-pillar analysis** of their environment + a group audit of where each pillar shows up |
| **3 — Systems that carry (and drop) knowledge** | Learns to map the actual delivery systems their organization uses to carry knowledge | Can run an environmental scan that surfaces where participation is assumed rather than designed, and where support is consistently dropped | **Environmental scan** mapping delivery systems and their structural gaps |
| **4 — Developing the research question** | Learns to narrow a broad curiosity into a specific, researchable question that names the most excluded | Can frame a question that is answerable within real constraints and centers the people the current system fails | **Refined research question**, pressure-tested with peers |
| **5 — Ethical constraints as design constraints** | Learns to treat resources, time, access, and consent as design constraints — consent as a relationship, not a form | Can scope a feasible investigation honestly and plan ethical engagement with marginalized participants from the outset | **Feasibility/resource map** + an ongoing informed-consent plan |
| **6 — The proposal as a living blueprint** | Learns to consolidate the inquiry into a responsive blueprint with clear success criteria | Can state objectives, methodology, and significance so any reader could judge whether the inquiry succeeded — and keep it adaptive | **One-page research proposal** (living blueprint) with success criteria |

## Phase 2 — Literature Review & Theoretical Scaffolding (Weeks 7–12)
*Goal: situate the inquiry within existing legal, social, and pedagogical frameworks.*

| Week | What the participant gains | What it enables in practice | Portfolio artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7 — Legal frameworks (the floor)** | Understands the legal baseline (ACA, AODA, ADA) as a floor, not a ceiling | Can audit their environment against the law and use legal literacy as a design tool — asking "who does this standard still fail?" | **Legal-frameworks audit** of their environment, naming what the law leaves out |
| **8 — Technical standards (the grammar)** | Gains working command of WCAG 2.1, while seeing that a checklist can't replace lived experience | Can run a WCAG scan on real content and spot content that passes the checklist yet stays pedagogically useless or alienating | **WCAG scan** of one piece of their own digital content |
| **9 — Disability justice (the compass)** | Learns intersectionality, leadership of those most impacted, and the social vs medical model | Can map power and voice in their context (who leads, benefits, is most impacted) and apply an intersectional lens to their question | **Power/voice map** + an intersectional analysis of their research question |
| **10 — Emergent learning (the method)** | Learns emergent learning's roots (critical theory, Freire) and the contrast with "banking" education | Can name where learning is already emerging in their context and design a condition that facilitates emergence rather than controlling it | **"Where learning is emerging" observation** + one designed condition that facilitates emergence |
| **11 — Neuroeducation & brain health** | Learns how active learning, spaced repetition, and emotional engagement support neuroplasticity | Can redesign a session with evidence-based practice and audit a design for "neurotypical-by-default" assumptions | **Redesigned session/module** (active learning + spaced repetition), audited for neurotypical defaults |
| **12 — Identifying research gaps** | Learns to synthesize the literature into a gap about missing integration of the three pillars | Can articulate the specific synthesis gap their work addresses — the most generative research position in the curriculum | **Gap statement**, stress-tested with peers |

## Phase 3 — Investigation & Methodology (Weeks 13–18)
*Goal: conduct field research to gather qualitative and quantitative data.*

| Week | What the participant gains | What it enables in practice | Portfolio artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **13 — Choosing methodology** | Learns to select a mixed-methods approach aligned to their question | Can defend a methodology that balances measurable scale with lived-experience depth, and state what counts as valid evidence | **Methodology choice + written defense** |
| **14 — Stakeholder engagement & co-design** | Learns to move from consultation-as-permission to genuine co-design | Can map stakeholders, see who's missing from feedback channels, and invite disabled learners in before decisions are final | **Stakeholder map** (with who's missing) + a co-design plan |
| **15 — Listening as a research practice** | Learns non-extractive, participatory listening with marginalized learners | Can run an interview/focus group that surfaces lived barriers and listen for the outlier data structured questions miss | **One participatory interview/focus group** conducted, with notes on what was heard |
| **16 — Quantitative data as provocation** | Learns to use surveys to surface patterns, treating numbers as provocation, not conclusion | Can design and pilot a short survey and audit it for able-bodied/neurotypical bias | **Three-question survey** designed and piloted |
| **17 — Ongoing ethical oversight** | Learns to treat ethics as an ongoing relationship — confidentiality, anonymity, renegotiated consent | Can maintain accountability and transparency as the research direction shifts | **Reviewed consent/confidentiality protocol** + an accountability mechanism |
| **18 — Collaborative inquiry** | Learns the value of peer-based collective sensemaking | Can host a preliminary-findings session that cross-pollinates ideas and identify which findings survive multiple perspectives | **Hosted preliminary-findings session** + the robust patterns it surfaced |

## Phase 4 — Analysis, Design & Synthesis (Weeks 19–24)
*Goal: translate findings into inclusive learning content and finalize the thesis.*

| Week | What the participant gains | What it enables in practice | Portfolio artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **19 — Meaning-making (data analysis)** | Learns thematic and statistical analysis that doesn't flatten lived experience | Can code qualitative data with a peer and produce findings that are both deep and broad | **Coded data / thematic analysis** (done with a peer) |
| **20 — Cross-case analysis** | Learns to distinguish a local problem from a structural pattern of exclusion | Can compare findings across contexts and generate insights that hold across environments | **Cross-case analysis** naming shared systemic patterns |
| **21 — Backward design for social change** | Learns to start from the end goal — social transformation and resilience — and design backward | Can write an end-goal statement defined by the most marginalized learner and align every choice to it | **End-goal / transformation statement** to design backward from |
| **22 — Prototype development** | Learns to build a rough, testable, born-accessible artifact applying UDL from the first draft | Can produce a prototype and surface invisible design assumptions by testing with someone typically excluded | **Accessible prototype / sample lesson** (UDL-applied), tested with a real person |
| **23 — Iteration & public feedback** | Learns to treat feedback as part of the design, via reflection-in- and on-action | Can run a structured feedback session with original stakeholders and refine while honoring their insights | **Documented feedback session** + recorded refinements |
| **24 — Culmination & dissemination** | Learns to return knowledge to the community in an accessible, living format | Can finalize and share a usable resource that keeps adapting beyond their control — completing the move to practitioner-researcher | **Finalized body of work**, shared as an accessible living resource |

## 6. The portfolio at a glance — how the trail rolls up

Read down the artifact column and the dossier assembles itself in four movements:

1. **The problem, defined** *(Weeks 1–6)* — Statement of Intent → environmental scan → research question → feasibility map → living proposal. A reader can see exactly what is being investigated, for whom, and why it matters.
2. **The problem, grounded** *(Weeks 7–12)* — legal audit → WCAG scan → power/voice map → emergence observation → redesigned session → gap statement. The inquiry is now situated in law, technical standards, justice, and pedagogy, with a precise synthesis gap named.
3. **The evidence, gathered** *(Weeks 13–18)* — methodology defense → stakeholder/co-design plan → interview → survey → ethics protocol → findings session. Real qualitative and quantitative data, collected accountably and tested with peers.
4. **The intervention, made** *(Weeks 19–24)* — thematic analysis → cross-case patterns → end-goal statement → accessible prototype → iteration log → public living resource. Findings become a born-accessible learning artifact, refined with the community and returned to it.

**What the participant ultimately holds:** not a certificate, but a complete, defensible, public body of work that demonstrates they can take a high-stakes accessibility problem from first observation to a tested, inclusive intervention — the evidence of a practitioner-researcher, not a content consumer.

---

*The recurring artifacts above map directly onto the ten Fieldwork competencies: the legal/WCAG/prototype work evidences the Structural cluster, the methodology/analysis/reflection work evidences the Dynamic cluster, and the power-mapping, co-design, and community-return work evidences the Relational cluster that the rest of the series treats as its ethical core.*
